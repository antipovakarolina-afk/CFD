Module 01

What is a CFD?

A Contract for Difference (CFD) is an agreement between a trader and a provider to exchange the difference between an asset's price when the contract is opened and when it is closed.

With a CFD, you do not own the underlying share. You speculate on its price movement.

Key terms glossary

Underlying asset

The share, index, commodity or other market whose price a CFD tracks.

Leverage

Exposure to a larger position than the cash margin you put down. It magnifies gains and losses.

Margin

Funds required by the provider to open and maintain a leveraged position.

Spread

The difference between the provider's buy and sell prices.

Stop-loss

An instruction intended to close a position at a specified level; execution price is not guaranteed in all conditions.

Slippage

The difference between the expected execution price and the actual execution price.

Important risk notice

Education, not investment advice

CFDs are complex, leveraged products and carry a high risk of losing money rapidly due to leverage. They are not suitable for every investor. This website is for general education only; it is not financial, investment, legal or tax advice, and it does not recommend any provider, asset or trade.

All examples and tool outputs are hypothetical and simplified. They exclude costs such as spreads, commissions, overnight financing, currency conversion, slippage and taxes unless stated otherwise. Actual contract specifications and protections vary by provider and jurisdiction.

Before considering any CFD, understand the product, read the provider's risk disclosure and check whether the provider is authorised by the relevant regulator. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose. Consider practising in a demo account and seeking independent advice.

CFDs are restricted for retail clients in some jurisdictions and are subject to regulatory rules. Check current Central Bank of Ireland and European Securities and Markets Authority information before relying on any regulatory summary.
